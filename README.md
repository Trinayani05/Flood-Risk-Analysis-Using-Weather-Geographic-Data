# 🌊 Flood Risk Analysis Using Weather & Geographic Data

Flood Risk Analysis Using Weather & Geographic Data is a data science project focused on analyzing environmental, weather, and geographic factors to identify flood-prone areas and assess different levels of flood risk. Floods cause significant damage to human life, property, agriculture, transportation, infrastructure, and the economy. Changing rainfall patterns, rapid urbanization, and environmental changes have increased the importance of identifying areas that may be vulnerable to flooding at an early stage. This project aims to develop a data-driven approach for flood risk prediction and visualization to support better disaster preparedness and decision-making.

The project analyzes different types of data, including rainfall, temperature, river water levels, soil moisture, and geographic information. Additional environmental and geographic factors such as elevation, land-use patterns, drainage networks, and satellite-based information can also be used to understand how different conditions contribute to flood risk. The collected data undergoes preprocessing, including data cleaning, handling missing values, feature engineering, normalization, and data integration.

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) is used to identify rainfall trends, seasonal patterns, relationships between weather variables, and the connection between geographic characteristics and flood occurrences. Visualizations such as rainfall distribution graphs, precipitation trends, correlation heatmaps, river-level analysis, and flood occurrence patterns help provide meaningful insights from the data.

Machine learning techniques are applied to analyze the identified patterns and predict flood risk. Models such as Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost can be used to classify areas into different flood-risk categories, including Low, Moderate, High, and Very High risk. The overall project workflow follows a structured approach of Data Collection, Data Preprocessing, Exploratory Data Analysis, Machine Learning, and Flood Risk Prediction.

The results of the analysis can be presented through data visualizations and geographic flood-risk maps. Tools such as Python, Power BI, Tableau, Streamlit, and GIS technologies can be used to visualize flood-prone regions, weather patterns, and risk levels. The expected outcome of this project is a comprehensive flood risk analysis system that can help identify vulnerable areas, support early warning systems, and assist disaster management and urban planning.
